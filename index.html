What the clock is worth

Rent commencement is set for Jan 26, 2027. Every 15 days the landlord is late credits the tenant roughly $38,634.92 in free rent — four of those blocks puts 60 days at $154,540. That is what a stalled permit costs, and it is why the ball is never allowed to sit.

4  dropped balls caught before they hit the schedule

SaveElectrical. Flagged for shared neutral conductor sizing on E1-201 — harmonics compliance not shown. Corrected and resubmitted; review passed.
SaveFire. Denied on two findings, including fire extinguisher travel distance. Corrected and resubmitted; review passed.
StealPlan review. Denied twice across two review cycles — resubmitted 8/10 answering the plan examiner's objection comments, then kicked back again and held 9 days with the ball still in the examiner's court. Caught and pushed back for reapproval before the clock ran; building review cleared its second cycle and the permit was approved.
SaveEng/Zone and Impact Fees. Both cleared the same week the others were denied — two lanes closed for good, so neither can block this build again.

Rent does not start when the work finishes. It starts on the date the lease says the space will be delivered — and past that date the tenant is credited free rent in fixed blocks. Every block is money the building was supposed to earn and does not.

Alongside the files sits the possession history: who held each item, when it moved, what they were sent, what came back, and how long it sat — every approval, denial and change order timestamped against the stakeholder who owned it.

Sooner or later somebody says the sentence: “I’m waiting on the engineer.” Or the architect. Usually it is true — and that is exactly the problem, because without a record it is unanswerable either way. The week goes by and nobody can say whose week it was.

The record answers it in seconds — who holds the item, since when, and how many days it has sat. “Waiting on approvals” stops being a position and becomes a checkable fact — which clears the contractor who genuinely is waiting just as fast as it surfaces the item nobody picked up. And when a change order is contested or a delay claim lands, the argument is settled the same way: most projects go hunting through inboxes months later to assemble that. On this one it already exists, finished, the day the job closes.

Does my GC, architect or engineer have to log in and use this too?

Each stakeholder is invited individually when the job is set up, and whether their participation is required or optional is the project lead’s call. Either way, Court Vision fits how your team already works — it doesn’t ask anyone to change how they operate.

How long does setup take on a new project?

Typically two to three weeks, depending on the size and complexity of the build. The lease details — rent commencement date, credit terms, key dates — get loaded along with the plan set and schedule, every stakeholder signs on, and the board is live and tracking before your first major milestone.

What happens to our project data if we ever stop using Court Vision?

It’s yours. At closeout you get the full record — every approval, submittal, permit and inspection, plus the closeout film — exported and handed to you directly. If you’d rather it came off our systems after that, we do it on request. Most owners keep the export: construction-defect claims can surface for years after a Certificate of Occupancy, and this record is usually the best evidence either side has. We just ask for a quick video testimonial in return, verified with your title and company.

Does this replace my project manager?

No. Court Vision runs alongside whoever manages the job on your side — a professional PM or one of your own in-house employees, whichever you choose. It carries the record and the alerting, so nothing sits on a desk no matter who’s holding it. You don’t have to bring in outside PM expertise just to keep a build moving. Whatever question you have, whoever’s running the job has the record on hand to answer it, on the spot.

What if the project’s scope or timeline changes mid-build?

The board gets updated to match the new signed agreement, so the tracker always reflects the real deal, not the one you started with. A significant scope change is scoped and quoted separately at that point.

Will Sky’s alerts spam my whole team?

No. Sky alerts whoever is actually holding the ball, by email, the moment something’s waiting on them — it’s their item on the dashboard and their responsibility to move it. Everyone else stays out of the noise. The nudge repeats periodically, to that one person, until the item is resolved — it doesn’t go quiet just because it’s been asked once.
